How the math works

  • Pension = age factor × years of service × salary average, plus any RetirementPlus bonus, capped at 80% of that average.
  • Tier 1 (joined before April 2, 2012): eligible with 20 years at any age, or at 55 with 10 years. Age factor is 2.5% at 65+, dropping 0.1 point per year younger (2.0% at 60, 1.5% at 55). Uses a 3-year salary average.
  • Tier 2 (April 2, 2012 or later): eligible at 60 with 10 years. Age factor runs from 1.45% at 60 to 2.5% at 67 (1.625% to 2.5% with 30+ years). Uses a 5-year salary average.
  • RetirementPlus: with 30+ years (20 of them teaching), add 2% for each full year over 24 (Tier 1) or 23 (Tier 2). That's +12% or +14% at 30 years. Still capped at 80%.
  • COLA: voted by the Legislature each year. Recent years: 3% of the first $13,000, so $390 max. The calculator adds it starting in your second year of retirement.
  • Options: B is about 1% less than A; C is roughly 9–11% less depending on ages. We use 1% and 10%.
  • No, Massachusetts doesn't have a "Rule of 90." The age-factor table is the whole show.

Assumptions & fun facts

  • You keep teaching full time every year until you retire, with steady raises. No buybacks, leaves, or part-time years.
  • Dollars are future dollars, not adjusted for inflation.
  • MTRS pensions aren't taxed by Massachusetts, but the IRS still wants its cut.
  • Teaching pay here doesn't earn Social Security. Since the Social Security Fairness Act (January 2025) repealed WEP and GPO, Social Security you earned elsewhere, or as a spouse, is no longer cut because of your pension.
  • The "odds" use a rough average-American survival curve. Teachers tend to beat it.

The not-funny part

This is an unofficial estimate for fun and planning, not advice, and not from MTRS. Get real numbers from your MTRS member account or a pre-retirement counselor. Sources: MTRS retirement overview, Tier 1 vs. Tier 2, MTRS COLA history, SSA on WEP/GPO repeal.
