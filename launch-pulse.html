    Double the mass

    Run with m = 0.50 kg. Predict whether a 1.00 kg block launched by the same pulse rises higher, lower, or to the same height, and by what factor.

    Check your reasoning

    The force graph is the same, so the impulse and the block's momentum after the pulse are the same (compare the momentum arrows). With twice the mass, the block leaves at half the speed. Height depends only on launch speed, and it goes as speed squared, so the block reaches one quarter of the height.

    Steeper incline

    Predict how the maximum height changes when the incline goes from 25° to 55°.

    Check your reasoning

    The normal force is perpendicular to the block's velocity, so it does no work, and gravity is internal to the block–Earth system. The block's kinetic energy all becomes gravitational potential energy, so the height is the same. On the steeper incline the block slides a shorter distance and slows down faster.

    Same area, different shape

    Compare a short, strong pulse with a long, weak one. Which launches the block faster?

    Check your reasoning

    Both triangles have area F0t0 = 1.2 N·s, so they deliver the same impulse, the same launch speed, and the same height. The peak force does not matter by itself; the area under the force–time graph does.

    Halfway through the pulse

    Turn on Pause at key moments and launch. At t = t0, what fraction of its final speed does the block have? What fraction of its final kinetic energy?

    Check your reasoning

    At t0 the block has received half the area, so it has half the impulse and half the final speed. Kinetic energy goes as speed squared, so it has only one quarter of the final kinetic energy. The launcher does less work early in the pulse because the block is moving slowly while the force is being applied (P = Fv).
